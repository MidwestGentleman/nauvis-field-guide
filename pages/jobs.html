Gathering, jobs and crafting

Each character has 11 jobs that level on their own, separate from character level. Three jobs gather raw resources and eight craft. Crafting is a gamble at first, so plan for failures.

How it fits together

Every craft needs materials, and there are two ways to get them:

  • Gathering: walk up to a resource node (wheat, quartz, mushrooms…) with the right tool and harvest it. This levels your gathering job: Farmer, Miner or Herbalist.
  • Hunting: win fights against mobs. This levels your character, and the mobs drop materials like branches, hides, shards and water.

Crafting turns both kinds of material into gear, tools, keys, potions and food. Each craft levels the job that made it. Higher job levels unlock better resources and bigger recipes, and raise your success chance. So gathering and hunting feed crafting, and crafting pushes your jobs up for the next tier.

The 11 jobs

Jobs, stats and levels belong to each character. Your inventory and marketplace kiosk are shared across all your characters. Job XP goes to the character who gathers or crafts.

Gathering

  • Equip the right tool, walk up to a node and press F. Any hoe works for farming; the tool's level doesn't matter.
  • Each resource has a job level gate: tier 1 needs level 1, then tier 2 needs 10, tier 3 needs 20, and so on up to tier 11 at 100.
  • You stand still while gathering: 12 seconds per node at level 1, falling to about 2 seconds near level 100. Yield per node grows with your job level.
  • Each gather gives job XP of 10 + half the resource's required level.
  • Each gather has a 0.1% chance of a bonus golden rare (golden wheat, golden mushroom, infinity quartz…). Golden rares are pet-food ingredients and sell well.

Protector ambush, 2% per gather. Sometimes the resource's guardian appears. You keep your harvest, but you're rooted until you fight it alone. Tier-1 protectors are level 1–5. A win always drops a bag of exactly 50 of that resource, worth 10–15 normal gathers.

Resource tiers

Crafting

  • Success chance = 50% + 0.5% per job level above 1, capped at 99%. That's 50% at level 1, 54.5% at 10 and 74.5% at 50.
  • A failed attempt burns every ingredient, but still gives job XP.
  • A recipe that is 4 or more ingredient slots below your current maximum gives no XP. Move on to bigger recipes as you level.
  • Stackable items (powders, food, keys) can be crafted up to 1,000 attempts in a single transaction. Gear is one attempt per transaction.
  • Gear rolls random stats inside a range when it's crafted, so a re-craft can roll better. You can wear an item once your character level is at least the item's level.

Water

There are no Water nodes. Water only drops from mobs, and most refining recipes need it (2 raw + 1 water). Good sources: Lorito Nakwa around Thebes, Misui Nakwa in rainforests, and Crabs in deserts. "Nakwa" in a mob's name means water.

Planning a craft

  1. Find the item on the recipe map

    Search for it on the recipe map. It lights up every ingredient behind it, down to raw resources and mob drops.

  2. Check the job gates

    Each sub-recipe has its own job and level. Your first Gilded Lorito key, for example, needs Tailor 10 and Jeweler 10.

  3. Divide by your success rate

    At 50%, plan two attempts per craft at every step of the tree.

  4. Subtract what you own

    What's left is your shopping list: what to gather, hunt or buy.

The craft planner does this for you. Pick an item and how many you want, and it lists the raw materials (with extra for failed attempts) and the crafts in the order you'd do them.

Crushing and runes

You can crush unwanted gear. The item is destroyed and about 25% of each stat line comes back as runes (Ba, Pa and Ra sizes). The job that crafts an item can scribe a rune onto it: the rune is used up, and the item can gain the stat, gain it while losing another, or lose stats. Unlist an item before crushing it.

Every item you own holds a small storage deposit on Sui. Crushing an item, or using up a stack, returns about 99% of it, so your SUI balance goes up slightly after you crush something.
