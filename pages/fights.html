Your first fights

Fights are turn-based on a square grid. You have 6 AP for spells and 3 MP for steps each turn, and those numbers never grow with your level. Losing costs nothing but time.

Starting a fight

  1. Engage a group

    Walk to a visible mob group and click it. Each group is one fight, with up to 6 fighters per side. Friends in your party can join during placement.

  2. Place yourself

    Pick a starting cell from the blue cells, then press Ready. Each placement move is a transaction, so choose once. The fight starts when everyone is ready, or can be forced to start after 60 seconds.

  3. Take turns

    Players always act first, then mobs, interleaved by side size: 1 v 3 plays P M M M, and 3 v 3 plays P M P M P M. There's no initiative stat. Among players, whoever opened the fight goes first.

  4. End your turn

    A whole turn (moves and spells) is sent as one transaction when you end it. You have up to 45 seconds; after that, anyone can end your turn for you.

AP, MP and range

  • 6 AP, 3 MP for every class, at every level. Only gear and pets add more: the Fuwa Horn Torque gives +1 AP, the Beru pet +1 AP, the Corbac pet +1 MP. Higher spell ranks often lower a spell's AP cost.
  • Range counts steps on the grid, never diagonals. Some spells need a straight line; most need line of sight.
  • Every living fighter blocks line of sight, your allies included. Obstacles block it too; holes don't.
  • Area spells can be aimed at an empty cell, for example between two mobs to hit both. Some areas also hit your allies, and the spell lists say so.

Tackle

When you leave a cell next to an enemy, you make an escape roll of your Agility against theirs. If it fails, you lose a big share of your AP and MP. With 0 Agility you escape a Lorito about a quarter of the time. Agility only helps in steps of 10.

Casting spells doesn't trigger the roll; only leaving the cell does.

Elements and damage

Each spell line deals one element: earth, fire, water or air. Its damage is multiplied by your matching stat: Strength, Intelligence, Chance or Agility. Mobs have resistances per element, shown on each bestiary page. Positive resistance cuts damage (50% at most); negative resistance adds damage with no limit. Mobs whose name contains Nakwa are water, Stone or Terra earth, Magma or Fire fire, and Gale or Wind air. The full maths is on Stats, elements and damage.

How mobs think

  • Each mob goes for the nearest enemy it can reach and casts the first usable spell in its list.
  • Some mobs push or pull, such as boars and some Aragnes, which changes who is nearest.

Where mobs spawn

Around Thebes, mobs come alone or in pairs. Bigger packs appear further from the world centre, and mob levels rise with distance too. The regional spawn rules list what spawns where.

Healing and losing

  • You can't use food or potions during a fight.
  • When you lose, you keep every item and all your XP. Your HP drops to almost nothing and comes back at 1 HP per second, or faster if you eat.
  • There is no flee. Forfeit gives the same result as losing, with no XP and no loot.
  • In a dungeon, losing a room ends your run and your key is gone. Earlier rooms' loot is kept.

XP and loot

  • Each defeated mob rolls its loot table once, and the drops are dealt round-robin to the winners. More players don't make more loot. A teammate who died still counts if the team wins.
  • A mob's level scales its drop chances. The listed chance applies at the middle of its level range, with 80% of that at the bottom of the range and 120% at the top, up to a maximum of 100%.
  • The winning team's average Chance raises every drop chance a little: +6 Chance is about +1%.
  • Party XP is multiplied by 1.0, 1.1, 1.5, 2.3, 3.1 or 3.6 for 1–6 players, then split by level. Wisdom adds 1% XP per point.
  • XP falls off sharply when you're far above the mobs' level. A party only gets its bonus if its weakest member is at least a third of the strongest's level.
